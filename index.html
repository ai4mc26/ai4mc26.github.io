01 / Overview

From plausible structures to physical discovery.

AI4MC '26 brings together materials scientists, chemists, physicists, experimentalists, and machine-learning researchers to ask what it takes to move from capable models to reliable scientific systems.

01

Generative modeling

New molecules, crystals, pathways, and mechanisms.

02

Accelerated simulation

Learning across electronic, atomistic, and continuum scales.

03

Automated laboratories

Closing the theory-experiment loop with active decisions.

04

Materials discovery

From virtual candidates to synthesizable, useful matter.

05

Electronic structure

Predictive representations of electrons, energy, and reactivity.

06

Multifidelity data

Combining sparse experiments with simulation at many scales.

Models are getting larger.

Can discovery get faster, sharper, and more physical?

04 / Panel

One deliberately difficult question

How far are we from an AlphaFold moment for materials?

  1. 01

    Theory vs. experiment: when they disagree, what should an AI system trust?

  2. 02

    Data scarcity: can foundation models work when decisive measurements remain rare?

  3. 03

    LLMs: where do they genuinely help discovery, and where do they merely narrate?

  4. 04

    Physics priors: indispensable inductive bias, or a ceiling on what models can discover?
